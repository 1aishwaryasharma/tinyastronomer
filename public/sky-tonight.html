Which side of the Sun?

Planets shine by reflecting sunlight. Their angle from the Sun helps indicate whether to look in the evening or morning. This model gives a rough geometry guide, not a local visibility forecast:

✦ Try it On a clear night, planets usually shine more steadily than stars, though they can twinkle when they are low on the horizon or the air is turbulent. Slide the date forward to watch the fast inner planets race around the Sun.
About this guide This uses simplified circular mean orbits to estimate each planet's elongation: its angle from the Sun in our sky. It does not calculate your horizon, latitude, twilight, weather, or the planet's brightness, so verify the result with a location-aware sky chart before planning an observation.
Rough guideAccuracy & sources

The date changes approximate Sun–Earth–planet geometry in two dimensions. “Evening” and “morning” describe which side of the Sun a planet occupies in this model, not predicted rise and set times. Uranus and Neptune generally require optical aid; all results depend on observing conditions.
