A world half-lit

The Sun illuminates roughly the half of Earth that faces it; twilight softens the boundary. As Earth spins on its tilted axis, the boundary between day and night — called the terminator — sweeps across the surface, giving every place its turn in the light.

✦ Did you know?
Teaching modelAccuracy & sources

Earth’s tilt, the Moon’s orbital timing, and eclipse alignment use measured values. Body sizes and distances use separate display scales. Eclipse shadows show alignment, not a predicted path, width, duration, or local view. The tide and aurora overlays explain the main forces, but they are not forecasts.
